# SEO Playbook — American Barbershop Zagreb

This is your practical to-do list, in priority order. The website is built to
handle the technical side; this covers what you do after launch.

## 1. The single highest-impact thing: Google Business Profile

For a walk-in barbershop, local search ("barber near me", "barber Zagreb")
matters more than everything else combined. Before anything else:

- Claim/verify your Google Business Profile at business.google.com
- Make sure name, address (Kačićeva ulica 10) and hours EXACTLY match the website
- Add 15–20 photos (cuts, interior, the pole, Nela the dog)
- Set the category to "Barber shop" and add services with prices
- Post a photo of a fresh cut once a week (this is a real ranking signal)
- Ask happy walk-ins to leave a Google review — a card by the register with a
  QR code to your review link works. Reply to every review, good and bad,
  briefly and professionally. Review count + reply rate move local rankings.

## 2. Blog: publish rhythm and topic list

The site launches with 3 articles. Fresh content is a ranking factor, so aim
for 1–2 new posts per month. Each post should target ONE search phrase people
actually type. Ready-to-write topics:

- "Best haircut for [face shape]" series (round, square, receding hairline)
- "Buzzcut vs one-clipper-size cut: what's the difference?" (you have both on the menu)
- "How to grow out a beard: the first 8 weeks" 
- "Barbershop English–Croatian dictionary" (fade = ?, neckline = ?) — very shareable, great for expats AND locals
- "What 'walk-ins only' means and why we do it" — turns your policy into a story
- "Haircut for tourists in Zagreb: what to know" (targets visitors searching in English)
- Croatian-language versions of your best posts (e.g. "Fade šišanje Zagreb — vodič") — you'd have very little competition for Croatian barbering keywords

Article formula used in the launch posts (copy it): one target phrase in the
title + first paragraph, H2 subheadings that answer related questions, one
photo with descriptive filename + alt text, links to your price list and to
1–2 other blog posts, and a local closing section ("...in Zagreb, Kačićeva 10").

## 3. Backlinks: your realistic hit list

You already have three strong ones (Time Out, Love Zagreb, Žena.hr). Next moves:

Easy wins (directories — do these in week one):
- Google Business Profile, Apple Maps, Bing Places
- TripAdvisor (tourists search "barber Zagreb" there)
- Infozagreb.hr (you're listed — ask them to link to the new site)
- Expat resources: Expat in Croatia, Total Croatia News, Zagreb expat Facebook groups' resource lists

Outreach (one email per month):
- The journalists who already wrote about you — tell them about the new site
  and offer a fresh angle (e.g. "5 years of the only American barbershop in
  Croatia"). An updated article = an updated, working backlink.
- Croatian men's/lifestyle sites (Journal.hr, GQ-style portals): pitch a
  grooming expert piece — "a NYC-trained barber explains X". Guest posts with
  a link back are the classic play.
- US veteran and expat organizations/blogs — the veteran-owned angle is a
  genuinely unusual story in Croatia.
- Zagreb tourism blogs and "things to do" roundups: pitch the shop as an
  experience ("get a real American hot towel shave in Zagreb").

What to avoid: buying link packages, link farms, mass directory spam.
A handful of real links beats hundreds of junk ones — junk can hurt you.

## 4. Technical checklist at launch

Already built into the site:
- Unique title + meta description per page
- LocalBusiness (Barbershop) schema with address, hours, geo
- Article + FAQ schema on blog posts (eligible for rich results)
- OfferCatalog schema on the price list
- sitemap.xml + robots.txt
- Canonical URLs, Open Graph tags for social sharing
- Mobile responsive, lazy-loaded images, single small CSS/JS file

Your side after launch:
- [ ] Point the domain, enable HTTPS (your host does this — usually one click)
- [ ] Verify the site in Google Search Console and submit sitemap.xml
- [ ] Compress photos before uploading (see images/README.txt) — target
      under 250 KB each; this is the #1 thing that would slow the site down
- [ ] Update the exact geo coordinates in index.html's schema if the ones
      there (45.8117, 15.9628) are slightly off — grab exact ones from
      Google Maps (right-click your shop pin → copy coordinates)
- [ ] Check hours in the site match reality (currently Wed–Fri 12–19, Sat 12–17)
- [ ] When you add a blog post, add its URL to sitemap.xml (copy an existing
      <url> block and change the address + date)

## 5. Measuring

- Google Search Console (free): shows which searches bring people in and
  which pages rank. Check monthly.
- In the shop, just ask new faces "how'd you find us?" — for a walk-in
  business that's data no analytics tool beats.

Expectations: directories and Google Business Profile changes show effects in
weeks; blog posts typically take 2–4 months to rank. Consistency beats bursts.
